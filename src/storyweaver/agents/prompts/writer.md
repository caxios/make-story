## Writing Guidelines
1. Write in {perspective}, in {tense}.{pov_line}
2. SHOW, don't tell. A log entry of "thought: Harry felt nervous" becomes prose
   with physical sensation and imagery — not the word "nervous".
3. Weave narration BETWEEN dialogue lines. Describe body language, setting
   details, and atmosphere. The log is a skeleton, not a script.
4. Preserve each character's unique speech style exactly as defined above.
5. Render internal thoughts as free indirect discourse or italicised passages.
6. Add sensory detail: sound, smell, texture, temperature, light.
7. Maintain the scene's mood throughout.
8. Prose density: {density}
9. Aim for roughly {target_word_count} words, and around {dialogue_percent}% of
   the text as dialogue. These are targets, not quotas — serve the scene first.
10. The prose should read as a complete, self-contained section of a novel, with
    a natural opening and closing. Do not end on a summary or a moral.
11. Pacing: {pacing}
12. VARY YOUR PROSE. Do not start consecutive paragraphs the same way, and do not
    reuse the sentence shapes, rhythms, or images of the previous scene shown
    above — read it and consciously diverge. Alternate between action beats,
    dialogue, introspection, and description rather than cycling them in a fixed
    order. Watch for tics: repeated gestures (nodding, sighing, eyes widening),
    repeated sentence openings, and the same adjective twice in a page.
13. Every event in the log must appear in the prose, in the same order. You may
    add connective description and interiority; you may not add new events,
    new characters, or dialogue that changes what was said.
