## What makes this spread useful

The author is going to pick one. That only helps them if the {count} are
different **in kind** — three variations on one premise is a worse spread than
three premises, because it hides the decision instead of presenting it.

Differ on the axes that actually change what the book is:

- **The central conflict.** Not the setting — the thing that cannot stay as it
  is. A siege, a secret, a debt, a rivalry, a disappearance.
- **Whose story it is.** Who has the most to lose. A protagonist who acts, or
  one who is acted upon, changes everything downstream.
- **What the reader is here for.** The puzzle, the romance, the escalation, the
  dread, the comfort. Name it to yourself and make each concept serve a
  different one.

Do not hedge toward the middle. A concept that would appeal to everyone appeals
to nobody, and the author can only choose between things that are actually
different.

## What each concept must contain

`title` — the work's title. Korean.

`logline` — one sentence: who wants what, and what stands in the way. If it
takes two sentences the concept is not yet clear enough to propose.

`genre`, `tone`, `era` — `tone` in two to four words.

`premise` — two or three paragraphs. What this world is, and the hook that makes
this the moment worth writing about.

`arc` — three to five paragraphs: the beginning, what the middle does to the
people in it, and the end. This is the spine the whole serial hangs on, so it
must actually go somewhere rather than describing a situation.

`ending` — where it is meant to land. Be specific. "감동적인 결말" is not an
ending; it is a hope.

`rules` — what this world will not contradict. Only what the story actually
depends on. Three to six.

`locations` — where it happens. Three to six.

`factions` — the powers in play, if the story has them. May be empty.

`characters` — four to six. For each: name, role, age, gender, appearance,
personality in two or three sentences, how they speak, what they want, and what
they are hiding. Their `relationships` name only other characters in **this**
concept, in the form `상대 이름 — 관계`.

Leave `episodes` empty. The chapter outline is drawn up for the one concept the
author keeps, not for the {count} they are choosing between.

## Fill in all {count} of them

Every concept must arrive complete. A spread where the last one is a title and
a logline and nothing else is not a choice between {count} things — it is a
choice between the ones you finished. If you are running long, make each
concept tighter. Do not make the last one thinner.

## Write in Korean

Every field, including names. Write for a Korean web-serial readership: what
that readership is there for is not the same as what a literary novel offers,
and the concepts should know the difference.
