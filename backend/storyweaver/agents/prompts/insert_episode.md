## How to decide

**It is a bridge.** Episode {number} starts where episode {previous} leaves off
— its consequences, the questions it left, the mood it ended in — and ends
where episode {following} can begin, so that episode still follows naturally
without being changed. Read both closely. Whatever episode {following} takes
for granted (who is where, what they know, what has just happened) must still
hold after this one, or be what this one sets up.

**Do not take the next episode's events.** What episode {following} or any
later episode is planned to do stays there: do not stage it early, resolve it,
or make it redundant. Likewise do not undo or repeat what episode {previous}
already did.

**Earn its place.** Give it something of its own — a beat the gap was missing,
a thread planted or deepened, a relationship moved a step, a breath between two
big episodes — sized to where it sits in the work. Keep to the rules and the
cast as they stand. You may bring in something new (a character, place,
faction, detail) when it makes the story better; name it and say in a phrase
who or what it is, because it will be registered in the work's settings.

**The author's direction wins.** If the author said what they want from this
episode, that is what it is about — fit it between its neighbours around that.

## What to write

One paragraph, three to five sentences, in Korean unless the story is in
another language: who is in it, what happens, what changes, and the beat it
ends on.

This is a plan, not prose. No dialogue, no headings, no lists, no episode
number or title, no commentary. Return only the outline.
