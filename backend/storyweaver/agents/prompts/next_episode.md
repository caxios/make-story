## How to decide

**Follow on.** Episode {number} starts from where episode {previous} leaves off:
the consequences of what just happened, the questions it left, the mood it ended
in. A reader who just finished episode {previous} should feel this is the
obvious next thing to read.

**Move the story toward where it is headed**, one step — a step the size this
point in the work calls for. Read where the episode sits: which part it is in,
what that part is for, and how many episodes are left. Stay inside the current
part's job; do not start the next part's, and do not approach the ending while
most of the book is still ahead. No one acts on knowledge of the ending, and
nothing is brought forward to get there faster.

**Use the threads.** If the layout says a thread is due to be planted around
here, plant it; if one is due to be paid off, this is where it pays off. Threads
planned for later are not answered now — they may be reminded of, or deepened.
Otherwise pick up one or two of the open threads, especially one that has gone
quiet for a while. Do not try to touch all of them.

**Build on what exists, and add when it is better.** Use the cast and the world
as written above, and keep to the rules as they stand. You may also bring in
something the plan did not have — a new character, even an important one; a
place, a faction, a detail of the world or of someone's past; a turn in where
the story is going — whenever it makes the story more interesting. Whatever
you add is registered in the work's settings, so introduce it clearly: name
it, and say in a phrase who or what it is.

**The author's direction wins.** If the author said what they want from this
episode, that is what it is about — fit everything else around it. If they said
nothing, choose what a good serial would do next.

## What to write

One paragraph, three to five sentences, in Korean unless the story so far is in
another language: who is in it, what happens, what changes, and the beat it
ends on — ideally something that makes the reader want the next episode.

This is a plan, not prose. No dialogue, no headings, no lists, no episode
number or title, no commentary. Return only the outline.
